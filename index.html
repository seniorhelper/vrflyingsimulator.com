How to use this flight simulator as flight school prep

A home simulator won't teach you the feel of a real airplane, and it doesn't count toward your logged flight hours. What it can do is make every paid hour count, because you won't be learning where the switches are while the Hobbs meter runs. Here is the approach Zach recommends:

  1. Learn the controls and instruments. Fly Lessons 1 through 5 in the 172-class trainer until the attitude indicator, airspeed and altimeter feel natural.
  2. Practice procedures, not just flying. Say your checklists out loud: before takeoff, climb, cruise, descent, before landing. Use the traffic pattern lesson to learn the legs and where to configure.
  3. Study for the written test now. The private pilot knowledge test is 60 multiple-choice questions, you get 2.5 hours, and you need 70% to pass. Passing it before or early in training saves a lot of money.
  4. Read the Airman Certification Standards (ACS). It lists every task and tolerance on your checkride, such as holding altitude within ±100 feet. Practice those tolerances here.
  5. Book a discovery flight. Fly with a real instructor, then come back and practice what you learned.

What does it take to get a private pilot license?

Under 14 CFR 61.109, a private pilot certificate for airplanes requires at least 40 hours of flight time, including 20 hours with an instructor and 10 hours solo. The dual training includes 3 hours of cross-country, 3 hours at night (with a 100-nautical-mile night cross-country and 10 night takeoffs and landings to a full stop), 3 hours of instrument training and 3 hours of checkride preparation. The solo time includes a cross-country of at least 150 nautical miles with full-stop landings at three points. You must be at least 17 to earn the certificate, 16 to fly solo as a student, and you'll need an FAA medical certificate (or qualify for BasicMed) and pass both the knowledge test and a practical test (the checkride). Most people need more than the 40-hour minimum, which is exactly why preparing first pays off.

Frequently asked questions

Is VR Flying Simulator really free?

Yes. It runs free in your web browser with no download, account or payment. You need a device and browser that support WebGL 2, such as recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

Can a flight simulator help me get my pilot license?

It can help you prepare, but it cannot replace real training. Home simulators like this one don't count toward logged flight time. They are excellent for learning controls, instruments, checklists, procedures and vocabulary, so your paid lessons with a certificated flight instructor go further.

How many hours does it take to get a private pilot license?

The FAA minimum for a private pilot certificate for airplanes is 40 hours of flight time under 14 CFR 61.109, including 20 hours with an instructor and 10 hours solo. Most students need more than the minimum.

How old do you have to be to fly a plane?

You must be at least 16 to fly solo as a student pilot in an airplane and at least 17 to earn a private pilot certificate. There is no upper age limit as long as you can hold the required medical certification.

Which airplane should a beginner fly first?

Start with the 172-class trainer. It is modeled on the most common type of training airplane, with a high wing, fixed landing gear and forgiving handling. Lesson 1 in the Training menu walks you through the controls.

Does the flight simulator work in VR?

Zach is the virtual flight instructor built into the simulator. He answers questions about flying, reads your instruments while you fly and briefs every lesson. His lessons are based on FAA handbooks and regulations, but he is not an FAA-certificated flight instructor.

Can I play it on my phone?

Yes. On touch screens you get an on-screen control stick, a throttle slider and buttons for gear, flaps, brakes, views and weapons. A computer with a keyboard, mouse or gamepad gives the most precise control.

What are the four forces of flight?

Lift, weight, thrust and drag. The wings make lift to balance the airplane's weight, and the engine and propeller or jet make thrust to overcome drag. In steady, level flight at a constant speed these forces are in balance. Change one, such as adding power or raising the nose, and the airplane climbs, descends, speeds up or slows down until they balance again.

Do I need a medical certificate to learn to fly?

You can take lessons with an instructor before you have one, but in the United States you need an FAA medical certificate before you fly solo in an airplane as a student pilot working toward a private certificate. Most private pilots hold a third-class medical, issued after an exam with an FAA-designated Aviation Medical Examiner. It is smart to get the medical early, so any questions come up before you have invested in training.

What is the FAA private pilot knowledge test?

It is the written exam for the private pilot certificate, taken on a computer at an approved testing center. It covers regulations, aerodynamics, weather, navigation, airspace and aircraft performance. You need an endorsement from an instructor or a completed home-study course before you take it. Studying the FAA's free Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge is a strong place to start.

What is a traffic pattern?

A traffic pattern is the standard rectangular path airplanes fly around a runway to take off and land in an orderly way. Its legs are called upwind, crosswind, downwind, base and final. Turns are to the left unless the airport publishes right traffic, and many piston airplanes fly the pattern at about 1,000 feet above the ground. Practicing the pattern in a simulator is a good way to learn the timing of each turn and checklist.
